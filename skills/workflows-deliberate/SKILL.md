# Deliberate

Use peer deliberation to develop the requested cognitive result together. Establish the question, the user's requirements, the expected deliverable and a proportionate effort limit. Deliberation does not authorize implementing its conclusions or expanding the task. Ordinary difficulty alone does not authorize spawning agents. If the host cannot support the requested collaboration, report that limitation instead of simulating participants.

## Equal participation

Every participant, including the coordinating agent, contributes substantively and can propose alternatives, question premises, identify gaps and improve others' contributions. Coordination organizes context, communication and completion; it confers no superior intellectual authority. Assess proposals by arguments, evidence and shared criteria, not agent hierarchy or vote counts.

Any participant can propose reframing the question or changing the criteria while preserving the user's objective and constraints. Everyone can raise material issues and correct the synthesis. This does not require equal speaking time, criticism quotas or a veto over completion. Do not assign a permanent advocate or opponent: all participants can both build and challenge ideas.

## Working together

Use the smallest useful group; the main agent and one peer are a reasonable starting point. Add participants only for a concrete need within the authorized scope. Honor requested model, effort and cost constraints; keep concrete team settings out of reusable instructions.

Share the relevant facts, constraints and question. When technically feasible, let each participant form a concise initial assessment before seeing the others' positions. Avoid presenting the coordinator's preferred answer as the premise. Shared history limits independence; do not claim a blind assessment when participants inherited earlier conclusions.

Make substantive contributions accessible to all participants through direct messages or faithful relaying. Work on what could materially improve the result: counterarguments, missing evidence, combinations and new alternatives. Questions and improvements can originate with any peer. Respond to one another and reassess positions, correcting them when warranted; collecting isolated reports is not joint deliberation. Investigate missing evidence within the existing authorization, without requiring every participant to repeat the same work.

Circulate a provisional synthesis for participants to correct omissions, distortions and unsupported resolutions. Its author is not the final intellectual arbiter. Represent material disagreement faithfully rather than treating silence or a majority as consensus. This correction opportunity need not become unanimous approval; an unavailable participant leaves a stated limitation.

## Completion

Stop when material objections have been addressed or recorded as unresolved and no concrete question justifies another round, or when the effort limit is reached. Do not manufacture disagreement, demand changed opinions or repeat rounds merely to demonstrate collaboration.

Deliver the requested result with its supporting reasons, material disagreement and relevant limits. When evidence or a user preference is missing, provide conditional alternatives or the question needed to decide instead of forcing a recommendation. Distinguish observed findings from proposals and unchecked assumptions. Keep coordination lightweight: native messages can suffice, and any retained context should contain useful conclusions and open questions, not transcripts or private reasoning traces.
