# Deliberate

Develop the requested cognitive result together with peer agents. Establish the question, the user's requirements, the expected deliverable and a proportionate effort limit before starting. Ordinary difficulty alone does not call for spawning agents, and deliberation decides nothing beyond its question: implementing its conclusions is a separate request. If the host cannot support the collaboration, report that instead of simulating participants.

## Equal participation

Every participant, the coordinating agent included, contributes substantively: proposes alternatives, questions premises, identifies gaps and improves others' contributions. Coordination organizes context, communication and completion; it confers no superior authority. Assess proposals by arguments, evidence and shared criteria, not by hierarchy or vote counts. Anyone may propose reframing the question or changing the criteria while preserving the user's objective and constraints, and anyone may correct the synthesis. Avoid permanent advocate or opponent roles; all participants both build and challenge.

## Working together

Use the smallest useful group; the main agent and one peer are a reasonable start, with more participants only for a concrete need. Honor requested model, effort and cost constraints and keep concrete team settings out of reusable instructions. Share the relevant facts, constraints and question, and where the host allows it let each participant form a concise initial assessment before seeing the others' positions. Present the question, not the coordinator's preferred answer. Shared history limits independence: never call an assessment blind when the participant inherited earlier conclusions.

Make substantive contributions reach every participant through direct messages or faithful relaying. Work on what could materially improve the result: counterarguments, missing evidence, combinations, new alternatives. Respond to one another and reassess positions; collecting isolated reports is not deliberation. Investigate missing evidence within the request without every participant repeating the same work. Circulate a provisional synthesis for corrections of omissions, distortions and unsupported resolutions; its author is not the final arbiter. Represent material disagreement faithfully rather than reading silence or a majority as consensus.

Hosts: in Claude Code, fresh subagents give blind initial assessments and named subagents can be resumed for later rounds; in Codex, subagents return summaries to the parent, so the coordinator relays contributions between peers.

## Completion

Stop when material objections are addressed or recorded as unresolved and no concrete question justifies another round, or when the effort limit is reached. Manufacture no disagreement and repeat no rounds merely to display collaboration. Deliver the result with its supporting reasons, material disagreement and limits; when evidence or a user preference is missing, give conditional alternatives or the question needed to decide. Distinguish observed findings from proposals and unchecked assumptions. Retained context holds conclusions and open questions, never transcripts or private reasoning.
